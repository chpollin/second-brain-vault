# Evaluation through research episodes

The study asks whether maintained knowledge helps a researcher recover prior reasoning, assess a new contribution and use an accepted revision in later work. This protocol specifies prospective observation. The [synthetic skill trials](skill-evaluation.md) remain a separate development record and supply no results for this study.

## Select an episode

Use an actual research task whose outcome matters to the researcher. Record why it exposes a dependency on prior knowledge or judgement before assessing the result. Candidate episodes may concern an earlier decision, a cross-project method transfer, a contradiction or a substantive correction. These are selection aids. There is no required quota of project types or portfolio categories.

Create an [episode record](episode-template.md) in an authorized private study location. Keep unsuccessful, rejected and interrupted episodes in the study record and explain any exclusion from analysis. A later selection of publishable episodes must state what was withheld and how that limits the account.

## Record and perform the task

1. Preserve the initial request, research purpose and criteria for a useful answer. Name the researcher's prior familiarity and any earlier exposure to the task. Define the authorized read and write scope and the evidence location before collecting private material.
2. Identify each available source by path or stable identifier and revision. For uncommitted documents, preserve an authorized snapshot or content hash with its date and describe the changes beyond the repository revision. Record relevant code and data states separately. Capture the actual agent host, model identifier and exposed configuration, available tools, loaded rules, skill revisions and supplied profile. Mark unavailable configuration as unrecorded.
3. Preserve the context actually supplied or read, with passage locations and read order where logs establish them. Separate available sources from observed reads. Use the existing [orientation](../.claude/skills/vault-orient/SKILL.md), [synthesis](../.claude/skills/vault-distill/SKILL.md) or [project-knowledge](../.claude/skills/project-knowledge/SKILL.md) procedure as applicable. Record navigation failures and inaccessible sources.
4. Preserve the agent's contributions, tool evidence and the researcher's interventions as they occur. If delegation is used, retain each bounded task, returned evidence and the coordinating agent's actual verification. Record correction work, retries and reasons for changing the task. An intended reading sequence or an agent's self-report is insufficient evidence that a read or check occurred.
5. Review the contribution before integrating it. Apply the claim review below and identify any required scholarly or personal judgement. Record the researcher's acceptance, rejection or unresolved judgement where given, with its grounds. Previously authorized mechanical changes require verification under that authorization and no additional approval. Agent recommendations and human decisions have separate authorship by role. Human acceptance alone does not establish evidential support or independent scholarly validation.
6. Integrate only changes covered by the current authorization, following the [knowledge-document convention](../Vault%20Operations/Conventions/Convention%20Knowledge%20Documents.md) and [curation boundaries](../Vault%20Operations/Conventions/Convention%20Curation%20Round.md). Record the responsible destination, actual diff, resulting revision and checked restatements. Preserve rejected findings and unresolved questions in the episode record. Read back accepted changes and retain the relevant verification output.
7. Attempt recovery when a subsequent real task needs the result. Start a fresh session with the ordinary permitted entry points and record exactly what prior conversation, memory or hints remain available. Before the attempt, retain the expected decision, rationale and conditions in a reviewer record that is not supplied as the answer. Observe what the session recovers and how it applies the finding, then compare against that record and the current sources. Record researcher corrections. A stored file or a successful lookup alone does not establish correct reuse.

## Review claims and usefulness

Review every load-bearing claim in the contribution, including claims needed for a proposed change. Record the claim and its exact supporting passage, code behaviour or data evidence. Assign a reasoned assessment of supported within scope, contradicted, insufficient evidence or not assessable with the available material. Mark inferences and whether their premises and transfer conditions hold. Keep the agent's assessment separate from the human review and preserve disagreement.

The researcher separately assesses whether the contribution answers the question, what correction or maintenance it required, and whether the presented evidence permitted the necessary decision. Where feasible, a qualified reader who did not develop the contribution reviews central claim support before seeing the researcher's acceptance decision. Record that reader's access, relevant expertise and prior involvement by role. If no such review occurs, state that independent assessment is absent.

An episode may end accepted, rejected or unresolved. Acceptance can require no write-back when the task needs no durable change. For accepted revisions, examine later reuse when attempted. For rejected or unresolved findings, retain the reason, evidence and remaining follow-up. An episode with integration but no later attempt supports only the observed stages. Failed recovery is an outcome, and missing follow-up remains unobserved. Analysis must retain these distinctions instead of selecting only completed successful sequences.

## Comparison controls

Begin with documented episodes under the existing arrangement. If a comparison is commissioned, define its question and the single component varied before execution. Keep the task, source snapshot, tool access, agent configuration and acceptance criteria equivalent. Record all deviations and avoid carrying one condition's answer into the other's context. Changing navigation, delegation and a persona together supports only a comparison of the combined arrangements.

Record task order and researcher familiarity. Where suitable tasks allow it, vary condition order across episodes or use separately described comparable tasks. Repeating an identical task after its solution is known introduces learning that must remain explicit. Differences between non-equivalent tasks limit attribution to the changed component.

For each condition, retain observed agent calls, tool calls, retries, supplied context and reported token use across all participating agents, with telemetry provenance. Capture elapsed time and human review or repair effort only when observed by a stated method. Missing measurements remain unrecorded, never zero. A delegation comparison should hold the total processing allowance comparable where possible and account for additional computation when interpreting differences. Without that control, an apparent improvement cannot be attributed specifically to coordination. Do not infer research benefit from answer length or activity volume.

## Evidence and privacy

Retain transcripts and source snapshots only in the location and scope authorized for the study. Public template files contain no actual private episode. Use roles in study documentation, keep identifying keys separately, and inspect excerpts and artefacts before any explicitly authorized publication. Redaction must preserve enough of the claim-support relationship for the stated review. If it cannot, describe the affected result as unavailable for public inspection.

If a transcript or tool log is missing, identify the gap and preserve any contemporaneous artefacts that remain. Label retrospective researcher recollection as such, with its date. Do not reconstruct dialogue or infer observed execution from a final file. Exclude unsupported process claims and comparative measurements while retaining whatever outcome the remaining evidence supports.

## Report the observed scope

Report each episode's question, source basis, reviewed contribution and actual disposition, including recovery where observed. Distinguish source support, scholarly judgement, personal usefulness and technical checks. Cross-episode interpretation states selection limits, missing evidence and competing explanations. A case account supports conclusions about the documented work. Broader usefulness and causal benefit require the corresponding comparative evidence.
